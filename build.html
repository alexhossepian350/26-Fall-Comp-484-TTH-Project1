Most experienced FPV pilots build their own drones. Building yourself means you know every part, you can fix it at the field when something breaks (and something will break), and you can tune it exactly the way you like. This page walks through the parts that make up a typical 5-inch freestyle quad, a little of the math behind how they work together, and the order you put them together in.

The parts

Parts list for a 5-inch freestyle quad
Part What it does Typical choice
Frame The carbon fiber skeleton that holds everything together and survives crashes. 5-inch “True X” carbon frame
Motors (x4) Brushless motors that spin the props. Size is stator width + height (e.g. 2207). 2207 size, 1950KV for 6S
Flight controller (FC) The brain. Reads the gyro thousands of times per second and tells the motors what to do. F7 processor running Betaflight
Electronic speed controller (ESC) Takes commands from the FC and delivers power to each motor. Usually one “4-in-1” board. 4-in-1, 55A, BLHeli_32/AM32/Bluejay firmware
Video transmitter (VTX) Sends the camera feed to your goggles over 5.8 GHz. Digital (DJI/Walksnail/HDZero) or analog
Receiver (RX) Picks up the control signal from your radio. ExpressLRS 2.4 GHz
FPV camera The camera you actually fly with. Low latency matters more than resolution. Matches your video system
Propellers Size × pitch × blades. Cheap, and replaced often! 5.1 × 3.6 tri-blade
Battery Lithium polymer pack. “S” is the number of cells in series (3.7V each). 6S, 1100–1300 mAh LiPo

The math

Motor speed:

RPM = KV × V

KV is how many RPM the motor spins per volt (with no load), and V is the battery voltage. A 1950KV motor on a 6S pack (22.2V) spins up to about 43,290 RPM.

Flight time:

minutes ≈ (C × 0.8 × 60) ÷ I

C is battery capacity in amp-hours and I is the average current draw in amps. The 0.8 means we only use 80% of the pack so the LiPo isn’t damaged. A 1.3 Ah battery at 20 A average gives about 3 minutes. FPV flights are short!

Thrust-to-weight ratio:

TWR = T ÷ W

T is the total thrust of all four motors and W is the all-up weight. Four motors making 1,500 g each on a 650 g quad gives a TWR of about 9 : 1, which is why freestyle quads feel so punchy.

Build steps

  1. Assemble the frame and mount the motors to the arms.
  2. Mount the ESC and flight controller stack on soft rubber grommets.
  3. Solder the motor wires and battery lead to the ESC.
  4. Connect the receiver, VTX, and camera to the flight controller.
  5. Use a smoke stopper the first time you plug in a battery, so a short circuit doesn’t fry anything.
  6. Flash and configure Betaflight (see Learning to fly).
  7. Install the props last, and only when you’re ready to fly.
