Learning to fly FPV takes time. Most pilots crash a lot at first, and that’s normal. The good news is that you can do almost all of that crashing on your computer. This page covers the ways most pilots take. I first started by practicing in a simulator, learned the flight modes, set up the flight controller, and finally go through a safety checklist before your first real flight.

Start in a simulator

Crashing in a sim is free. Crashing a real quad costs props, arms, and sometimes a lot more. Plug your radio into your computer over USB and put in at least 10–20 hours before your first real flight. There are tons of YouTube videos out their that will help with learning the fundementsals. Some simulators include:

Liftoff (my favorite)
A classic on steam, with lots of maps and realistic physics.
Uncrashed
Beautiful freestyle spots, great for practicing smooth lines.
VelociDrone
The go-to sim for racing and online multiplayer.

Flight modes

  • Angle mode: The drone levels itself when you let go of the stick. Good for your very first minutes.
  • Horizon mode: Self-levels near center stick, but lets you flip at full stick.
  • Acro mode: Full manual, no self-leveling at all. The drone holds whatever angle you leave it at. Almost every FPV pilot flies acro, so aim to switch to it quickly.

OSD warnings

The OSD (on-screen display) is text the drone prints on top of your video feed. It shows live numbers like your battery voltage 16.2V and flight timer 02:31, plus warnings when something needs your attention. These are the warnings you’ll want to recognize instantly:

ARMED
The motors are live. Stay clear of the props.
LOW BATTERY
Start heading back.
LAND NOW
The pack is almost empty. Land immediately.
RXLOSS
The drone lost your radio signal and failsafe is about to kick in.

Pre-flight safety checklist

Know the rules

In the US, recreational pilots must pass the free FAA TRUST test, register any drone over 250 g (and use Remote ID), stay under 400 ft, and have a visual observer watching the drone while they’re wearing goggles.

  1. Check the props for chips or cracks, and make sure they’re tight.
  2. Make sure the battery is fully charged and not puffy or damaged.
  3. Turn on your radio before plugging in the drone battery.
  4. Confirm you have video and that failsafe works before taking off.
  5. Fly away from people, roads, and airports.

Keep a flight log

After each session, write down what you flew and how it went. Looking back at your notes is a great way to see your progress and remember what to work on next time.
