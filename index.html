What is FPV?

FPV stands for First Person View. A small camera on the front of the drone streams live video to a pair of goggles on the pilot’s face, so you see exactly what the drone sees and fly it as if you were sitting in the cockpit. Unlike a camera drone that hovers in place with GPS, most FPV drones are flown completely by hand. There is no auto-leveling and no autopilot, just you, the sticks, and a lot of practice. The result is a kind of flying that feels closer to a video game or an extreme sport than to taking photos.

FPV has grown from a small hobby of people soldering drones together in their garages to a huge community of freestyle pilots, racers, and filmmakers. Those sweeping, diving shots through buildings and down waterfalls that you see in movies and car commercials? A lot of them are filmed with FPV drones.

Styles of FPV

Freestyle
Flips, rolls, and dives around trees, buildings, and abandoned spots. It’s all about smooth, creative lines, kind of like skateboarding in the air. Usually flown on a 5-inch quad.
Racing
Light, powerful quads flown through gates and flags on a tight course. Races are often won by tenths of a second.
Cinematic
Small ducted drones called cinewhoops carry an action camera for smooth, close-up video indoors and around people.
Long range
Larger, efficient 7-inch quads built to cruise over mountains and coastlines for several kilometers on a single battery.

How it works

An FPV drone has two separate radio links: video coming down from the drone to your goggles, and control going up from your radio to the drone.

  1. The camera captures video and sends it to the video transmitter (VTX).
  2. The VTX broadcasts the video on 5.8 GHz to your goggles.
  3. You move the sticks on your radio controller, which sends commands to the drone’s receiver.
  4. The receiver passes them to the flight controller, the drone’s brain.
  5. The flight controller tells the ESC how fast to spin each of the four motors.
